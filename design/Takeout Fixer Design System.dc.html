The five liveness signals
  1. Rotating arc — 1.1s linear, the only perpetual motion in the system.
  2. Determinate bar with a 2.4s sheen pass, so the bar moves even when the percentage doesn't.
  3. Filename ticker, re-rendered per file with a 140ms fade-up. This is the real anti-frozen device — it changes many times a second on small files and still changes on big ones.
  4. Elapsed counter, ticking every second regardless of progress.
  5. Running result tallies, which grow independently of the bar.
Rules that come with it
  • Never an indeterminate barber-pole. It's the universal signal for "we don't know", which is the one thing you must not say to someone waiting on their photos.
  • Never a spinner alone.
  • Estimate is coarse and honest — "about 3 minutes left", never "2:47". Rounded down after the first 30 files, hidden before that.
  • The stop button states what is kept. "Cancel" alone implies losing the 108.
  • The tallies use the notice slate for "can't be fixed", not warning amber — the user did nothing wrong.
  • Under reduced-motion the arc and sheen stop; bar, counter, ticker and tallies still move, so all five signals degrade to three real ones.

How the table degrades
  • Below 720px the grid becomes stacked blocks. Not a scrolling table, not a squeezed one.
  • Column headers become inline labels — "Date written" travels with its value, so the label is never off-screen.
  • Filenames wrap with word-break: break-all rather than truncating. A truncated filename is useless to someone checking whether the right file was touched.
  • "Date found" is dropped at 360px. It's derivable from "Date written" in the normal case and irrelevant in the failure cases — the one fact worth cutting.
  • Status word moves above the filename: it's the thing being scanned.
  • The filter chips scroll horizontally — the one horizontal scroll allowed, because chips are self-evidently a row.
  • 50 rows per page, not virtual scroll: a paged list has an end, and an infinite list of your own broken files does not.
  • Tile numbers drop from 39px to 25px. At 360px, 39px + a 3-digit number + a label doesn't fit on one line, and a wrapped tile label is worse than a smaller number.

You were right that button-press is the wrong moment — a press is already acknowledged by the pointer. Option 1 ships. The wait is where trust is lost, so that's where the one moment goes; option 2 is deleted, not held in reserve. It stays below for the record, and because its cost — a control changing semantic colour — is a rule worth keeping written down.

Option 1 — the tally tickLOCKED — SHIPPING

Where: during the multi-minute wait, on the three running tallies. When a number changes, the new number fades up 3px into place over 120ms, cubic-bezier(0.2,0,0,1). The old number does not animate out — it's replaced.

What it's worth: it converts the multi-minute silence — the moment where trust is actually lost — into visible accumulation. Tabular numerals mean the digits don't shift, so the motion reads as a value landing rather than text reflowing. It also does real work: it's the fifth liveness signal.

What it costs: it fires hundreds of times, so it must be throttled to at most one tick per tile per 400ms with counts batched in between, or it flickers on fast machines. And it bends your own rule — this is a repeating micro-state, not a single moment. If that bothers you, take option 2.

Reduced-motion fallback: numbers change with no transition. Nothing is lost — the counts still update, which was always the information.

Where: the instant the zip finishes writing. The primary button's icon and label crossfade to the success check and "Saved to your Downloads folder" over 160ms, ease-out, at a fixed width so nothing reflows. No colour change, no scale, no bounce.

What it's worth: it's genuinely one moment, once per session, and it answers the single most common question after any browser download — did that actually save, and where? That's information, not decoration, which is the strongest possible justification for an animation.

What it costs: it lands after the anxiety has already resolved, so its marginal value is lower than option 1's. It also changes the button's fill to success green, which is the one place in the system where a control changes semantic colour — a small inconsistency I'd be introducing on purpose.

Reduced-motion fallback: the label and icon swap instantly. Identical information, zero transition.

Either way, the wrapper is the same: the transition is declared inside @media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference) — opt-in rather than opt-out, so the static version is the default and the animation is the enhancement. The perpetual arc and progress sheen in §8 are separate: they're liveness, not signature, and they also stop under reduced-motion.

VERIFYneutral-300 control borders against neutral-50 and #ffffff — a UI boundary needing 3:1. The likeliest failure in the whole spec; the fix is 400.

EXEMPTDisabled controls (neutral-400 on neutral-200) fail on purpose and are exempt under WCAG. The checker should report them as EXEMPT, not FAIL, or you'll chase them forever.
EXEMPTThe brass tile bars. They're decorative — every number they encode is also written in text — so 3:1 is a nice-to-have, not a requirement. Report as EXEMPT with a note.
14 · Portable vs tuned

You named the tension yourself: this is a personal system for many repos, but every input describes one audience. Here's the split. The left column travels; the right column is a decision you should re-make for each product, and knowing which is which is what keeps this from silently becoming "the anxious-user system" everywhere.

You said you like portable — so the system ships in two pieces

The core is everything in the left column: colour, type, space, shape, states, icons, the accessibility rules. It's the thing you install in a new repo without thinking. A profile is a short, named document per product that records only its tuned decisions — Takeout Fixer's is the right column. A new utility starts with the core and an empty profile, and every entry it adds has to be argued for.

Three things I'd promote from tuned to portable, because they turn out to be rules rather than taste — they just need a stated trigger instead of an audience:

  • Progress detail scales with duration. Under 1s: nothing. Under 10s: a bar. Over 10s: add a per-item ticker. Over 60s: all five liveness signals. That's a threshold, not a personality — portable.
  • Confirm destructive actions when there's no undo. Ask twice if and only if the action is irreversible and the data is the user's. Also a condition, not a taste — portable.
  • Every failure state names what happened to the user's data. "Your file wasn't changed" is the shape; it applies to any tool that touches files at all — portable.

What stays firmly in the profile: tiles-over-table, "nothing is uploaded" above the fold, paged-not-infinite rows, and where the signature moment sits. Those are genuinely about this audience. The lists below are updated to match.

Portable — your brand
  • All eight ramps and both step-mapping tables.
  • Type scale, per-size letter-spacing, the system-only font decision, tabular numerals.
  • 4px spacing scale, 6px/10px radii, two elevations, the pill ban.
  • The single focus ring: 2px primary-400, 2px offset, on every control type.
  • The three button geometries and their five states.
  • Icon rules: inline SVG, 16px viewBox, 1.6px stroke, currentColor, never icon-alone.
  • Never-hue-alone: icon + word on every status, in every product.
  • Notice as a fifth semantic. Every tool you build has factual disclosures. This is the most valuable portable idea in the system.
  • Error copy shape: what happened, then what happened to the user's data, then the fix.
  • Progress detail scales with duration — the 1s / 10s / 60s thresholds.
  • Confirm destructive actions when there is no undo, and only then.
Tuned — re-decide per product
  • Summary-tiles-over-table. Right when the detail is a wall the user didn't ask for; wrong in a tool where the table is the product.
  • Which rung of the progress ladder you land on. The ladder is core; Takeout Fixer sits on the top rung because it runs for minutes.
  • "Nothing is uploaded" above the fold. That's the client-side promise, not a universal.
  • How much reassurance, and how often. Naming what happened to the data is core; repeating "nothing is uploaded" on three separate screens is this product.
  • Paged 50 rows instead of virtual scroll. A deliberate anti-infinite-list choice for anxious users; wrong in a power tool.
  • The signature moment's placement. The technique is portable; "during a multi-minute wait" is not.
  • The brass accent. Yours, permanently — but its role (measurement, never status) is the portable half.
Where I borrowed from Apple, in one place

Borrowed, knowingly: the cool grey neutral scale and negative tracking at display sizes (both Apple house style); icon + text label on every action, never icon-alone (Photos); summary-first with detail one click behind, and stripped distraction (News); a 6px control radius; system fonts as a first-class decision rather than a fallback.

Diverged, deliberately: teal instead of system blue, so blue stays available for info and notice; a fifth semantic Apple has no equivalent for; no translucency, vibrancy or blur anywhere, because they cost contrast you can't verify and GPU time on old machines; stroked icons at every size instead of Apple's filled-at-small-sizes pairs; brass as a measurement accent, which is not an Apple move at all; and a progress state built around five redundant liveness signals, where Apple would show one confident bar. That last one is the biggest divergence: Apple designs as though the machine will not disappoint you. Your tool runs on the user's hardware and sometimes will.

What B buys you

What B costs you

The photo problem. A warm surround shifts how a photo's own white balance reads — thumbnails of yellow-lit indoor shots will look worse than they are. For a photo tool that is a genuine cost, not a taste quibble. The brass problem. Yellow-greens have the narrowest usable contrast band of any hue; brass works as a dark interactive colour and as a pale tint, but the middle of the ramp is nearly unusable for text, and it sits uncomfortably close to warning amber — I'd have to push warning toward orange to keep them apart.
